First names in the collection

New work on a museum wall, new lines on a civilian grid

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~286 words

The National Gallery of Art in Washington, DC, has announced a raft of new acquisitions, about 190 works since March. Many of the names enter the collection for the first time. Marie Bracquemond. T. C. Cannon. LaToya Hobbs. Stacey Kranitz. Lorraine O'Grady. Christina Quarles. A first hanging is a kind of citizenship. The wall says they belong.

On Thursday, August 20, 2026, we hold that wall to one pressure: what we admit into the hold, and what we strap to the life that already lives there.

The FDA granted accelerated approval to an Ultragenyx gene therapy, the first treatment designed for the underlying cause of glycogen storage disease type Ia. A rare metabolism gets a door. Heidi Overton is the president's pick to run that agency. The Department of Homeland Security was sued over a policy that would limit how long foreign students and scholars can remain.

WIRED reports a war game on Volt Typhoon, a Chinese hacking group, and digital bombs strapped to civilian infrastructure. In Independence, Missouri, and other towns, voters move to oust officials who backed data centers. What we admit to the grid is not only a server. It is a town.

Katalyst Space and NASA abandon a plan to raise the orbit of Swift, citing attitude control problems with the Link spacecraft. A SpaceNews panel asks what comes next for intelligence from orbit. The sky is a sensor. The sensor can fail to hold.

The strap is not a theory. It is a pipe, a clinic record, a pad. Convergence is not a thicker wall. It is whether the names we let in, in a gallery or a clinic, still belong to the living hold and not only to the strap.
