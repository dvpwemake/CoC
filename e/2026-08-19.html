The unwanted still has a use

Graphite that keeps the dark, granite they would coat white

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~292 words

Alejandro Aguilar Canela draws in graphite and keeps the smudge. The Austin artist welcomes the mark that was not planned. There is another side to our own darkness, he says. It is more about not discarding what might be considered unwanted, but instead finding some use for it. His work is up at Ki Smith Gallery in New York through September 6.

On Wednesday, August 19, 2026, we hold that smudge to one pressure: what we would coat over, and what still has to remain in use.

A federal judge granted the Trump administration permission to proceed with paint tests on the Eisenhower Executive Office Building. The desire is to coat the granite facade with white paint. The grain of the stone is the thing they would discard.

In Independence, Missouri, and in other towns, voters move to oust local officials who backed data centers. The servers want land, power, water. The recall is a use the ballot still has. FORT Robotics, which builds a trust layer for physical AI, plans to go public through a business combination. The machine asks to be trusted. The town asks to be heard.

Heidi Overton is the president's pick to run the FDA. The Department of Homeland Security was sued over a policy that would limit how long foreign students and scholars can remain. Katalyst Space and NASA abandon a plan to raise the orbit of Swift, citing attitude control problems with the Link spacecraft. NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter images a new crater on the Moon, left by a Falcon 9 upper stage on August 5. Aircraft vapor trails still warm the air.

Convergence is not a whiter facade. It is whether the unwanted, in a drawing or a stone, still gets to be used.
