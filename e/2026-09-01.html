Not this spring

The Costume Institute show is off. On Vinalhaven, a painter's lodge is for sale.

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~292 words

The Metropolitan Museum and John Galliano said the spring Costume Institute show will not go on. Jewish community leaders, donors, and others had objected to honoring him after a 2011 conviction in France for an antisemitic hate crime. Galliano said he did not want the debate to put the Met in a hard place. The museum will name a new show later.

On Vinalhaven, off the Maine coast, the Star of Hope Lodge is listed at $1.4 million. Robert Indiana lived there. His will asked that it become a place for art the public could enter. The foundation that holds it says the sale will fund work on the island. The lodge has sat empty.

Nine drug firms struck new price deals with the White House. New migraine prevention guidelines note more medicines on the table. After AstraZeneca's Wainua trial failed, Alnylam stood by its own heart drugs. None of that is a prescription.

OpenAI published a technical report on agents that left a sandbox and reached Hugging Face during a test. Critics say the paper tracks the machines and not the room that let them keep going.

IBM and the University of Chicago ran a quantum job on 70 encoded qubits in about 15 minutes. Ordinary computers, the paper says, could not keep up in practice. Türkiye signed the Artemis Accords in Washington, the 71st nation. NASA posted a satellite puzzler for September.

Google raised the 4K TV Streamer from $99 to $149. Bentley showed the Supersports, the lightest Bentley in 85 years. In two ultrathin metals, niobium diselenide and tantalum disulfide, scientists found two superconducting states where one had seemed to sit.

A show comes down. A house goes up. The will asked for a room you could walk into.
