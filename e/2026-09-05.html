Photo: Long-Awaited Lucas Museum Receives Mixed Reviews Ahead of Opening

Cropped from the frame

Lucas opens to the press. Dana-Farber is off the plan.

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~309 words

The Lucas Museum of Narrative Art opened to the press in Los Angeles this week. George Lucas and Mellody Hobson built it for stories. The reviews split. Some saw a house of plot. Some saw a brand with a roof.

On Friday, September 4, 2026, we hold that opening to one pressure: who still gets into the story, and who is cropped from the frame.

Mass General Brigham had already split with Dana-Farber. Now its Medicare Advantage plan is dropping the cancer hospital. Ionis won FDA approval for Zanvastro, the first disease-modifying treatment for Alexander disease, a rare and fatal neurological disorder. The same week Novartis said pelacarsen, a closely watched heart drug, failed a pivotal study. A first. A miss. A network that lets a cancer hospital go.

Geles Cabrera, who forged a sculptural language of the human figure in Mexico, has died at 100. In New York, Erin Milez's Everything the Light Touches opens September 10 at Monya Rowe Gallery. She paints people packed onto beaches and subways, faces cropped, devices on, nobody speaking. She left New York for Seattle and started counting how close you can sit without looking up.

NASA will host a virtual Artemis webinar for the blind and low-vision community on the RS-25 engine. IEEE Spectrum describes electronics-covered cockroaches with spring-loaded needles, meant to reach people under rubble. The Verge points to field recordings you can work to, sleep to, or listen to as if they were the room. Audacity 4 rebuilt the world's most popular audio editor after a logo fight. Plasmalogens from edible sea squirts improved memory and synapses in aged mice. Mice. Not a prescription.

A museum for narrative. A painting of people who do not look. Convergence is not the opening. It is whether the plan still holds the person under the rubble, and the one the network dropped.
