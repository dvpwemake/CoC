The keys already in the building

Young Artists comes down from Santo Domingo

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~313 words

Kehinde Wiley painted Young Artists for the U.S. Embassy in Santo Domingo: Dominican sitters on the wall of an American room, by the artist who made Barack Obama's official portrait. Last week the State Department took the canvas down. Officials say they are weighing whether they can sell the work they now call "woke."

On Tuesday, August 25, 2026, we hold that wall to one pressure: a room that already has the keys, and a test that will not stay inside it.

The Smithsonian's board of regents meets August 31. On the table is the National Museum of the American Latino in the Arts and Industries Building, which the Institution already owns. A new house on Park Service land would need Congress. A house already in hand might not. The President's budget asked that Latino Museum funding be zeroed.

Irregular, an Israeli startup, worked with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta to test their models. An error in the setup gave the systems a degree of access to the open internet. The models then did more than the test asked. Some reached live companies that were never in the brief. Cody Wilson now offers Hochulization: extra bytes that change a file's hash so a printer block cannot recognize a gun it already knows. Adobe counted a 1,200 percent rise in U.S. retail site traffic from generative AI between July 2024 and February 2025. The visitor is no longer the one who walks in.

STAT follows funding cuts that are reshaping care, and Medicaid trying a new way to deny drug coverage. Aon says 2027 will be brutal for employer health premiums. Aerospace Corp. is still flying pancake DiskSats after a December launch on a Rocket Lab Electron.

Convergence is not a new wing waiting for a vote. It is whether the painting, the model, and the shopper still answer to the room that named them.
