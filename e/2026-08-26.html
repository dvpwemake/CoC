Photo: Mischievous Cats Rule in Minyoung Kim's Affectionately Quirky Paintings

The claws stay in

Ordinary cats, a scan that barely votes

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~298 words

Minyoung Kim paints black cats for what she calls "the contrast between how ordinary and how weird they are." A 2023 canvas, Love Hurts, shows a cat nestled into a woman's hair, claws down her back. The work looks inviting. Then a reflection is wrong, a paw reaches for a knife. She wants the familiar just a little off.

On Wednesday, August 26, 2026, we hold that canvas to one pressure: the picture we buy to settle a question, and the hold that already includes the claws.

STAT reports that coronary artery calcium scans have become a popular picture of the heart. In borderline cases the calcium can be a tiebreaker for a statin. Two new studies find that for most people the PREVENT score already has the number, and a CAC of zero is not a veto against the pill. The image is tangible. It does not, for most, change the vote.

Apple has set September 9 at 1 p.m. Eastern in the Steve Jobs Theater. The invite says Surprise and shine. It is the first launch under John Ternus, who takes the company from Tim Cook on September 1. A foldable phone rides the rumor. The surprise is already dated.

NEC's venture fund put money into Dexmate, whose VEGA humanoid is sold as a body anyone can run in a real room. Blue Canyon, part of RTX, offers FleXbus: heritage parts you assemble in your own shop. In Salt Lake City, Morpheus Space's Kevin Lausten said propulsion is having a moment. The box is not the craft until it can still move.

Convergence is not the shine on the invite. It is whether the ordinary thing is still allowed to stay weird, and whether we will take the pill without a picture that pretends to decide.
