Shifts in Authority

From the FDA's New Era to Tech Brands Fading

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~336 words

As the Senate scrutinizes the next FDA chief and Microsoft drops its AI-branded PCs, global developments in energy, art, and space highlight a world in transition.

The geopolitical and technological plates are shifting. In Washington, FDA commissioner nominee Heidi Overton faced Senate scrutiny, dodging heated questions on vaccines and abortion while clearly outlining her core priorities: accelerating biomedical research and countering China's biotech rise. (The global biotech pulse remains a focal point, as noted in recent dispatches from Shanghai.) Meanwhile, in the tech sphere, Microsoft is quietly killing off its "Copilot Plus PC" brand just two and a half years after pushing it as the ultimate AI hardware standard. Across the Atlantic, U.S. federal officials are intervening on behalf of Elon Musk’s X to fight a $140 million European penalty regarding deceptive site behavior.

Infrastructure, exploration, and discovery are also seeing major updates. Domestically, the DOE just injected $1.9 billion into advanced grid technologies to squeeze more capacity out of an overstressed U.S. network. Looking upward, NASA welcomed Côte d’Ivoire as the 75th signatory of the Artemis Accords, marking a major milestone for the international space coalition. On the ground in Guadalajara, Mexico, an IEEE team is expanding access to STEM by building portable educational robotics platforms for talented high school students. Further advancing scientific understanding, Weill Cornell researchers discovered how the PIP2 lipid governs retinal light sensitivity, opening new therapeutic pathways for retinal degeneration.

In the art world, leadership and legacy remain in sharp focus. Jonathan Abbott brings 15 years of public media experience from Boston's GBH to his new role as director of MOCA Los Angeles. In Paris, courts are deliberating whether a Pissarro painting auctioned at Sotheby's was looted by Nazis, weighing claims from the heirs of Jewish banker Salomon Flavian. Finally, we bid farewell to Brazilian painter Lucia Laguna, who passed away at 85. Retiring from teaching before ever picking up a brush, she reminded us that true artistic success—much like her verdant, fragmented cityscapes—can beautifully emerge in our 60s and beyond.
