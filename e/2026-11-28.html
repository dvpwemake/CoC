Pigments, Pills, and Orbital Power

From Vermeer doubts to psilocybin speed, surface appearances rarely tell the whole story.

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~298 words

Today’s dispatches ask us to look past the surface—whether examining a master’s brushwork, structural color, or the geopolitical realities unfolding above the atmosphere.

In the art world, canonical certainty just took a hit. A Vermeer specialist is casting doubt on the authenticity of two celebrated works, The Love Letter and The Glass of Wine, arguing neither came from the Dutch Old Master’s hand. Elsewhere, institutional ambition marches forward: Hoor Al Qasimi will curate the 2028 Venice Biennale, while politicians crowded the aisles of Art Taipei alongside 127 galleries ahead of local elections.

On the material front, scientists have engineered a glossy, automotive-grade paint that never fades—without using a single drop of pigment. Inspired by nature, the coating relies on microscopic silicon spheres to manipulate light structurally rather than chemically.

Medicine offers a striking study in contrasting tempos. In neuroscience, a single dose of psilocybin relieved severe depressive symptoms within 48 hours, with benefits lasting over three months. Yet conventional healthcare moves far slower: the FDA has extended its assessment of Vertex’s investigational kidney drug over safety questions, and patients in Boston—America’s purported medical capital—still face grueling waits just to see a primary care doctor.

Above Earth, nostalgia meets hard power. NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day looks back in stereoscopic 3D at the Apollo 17 VIP site, a reminder of peaceful lunar exploration. Today’s reality is decidedly sharper: a Novaspace report reveals global governments poured $71.8 billion into space defense and security last year as orbital militarization accelerates.

Back on the ground, automation is accelerating too. XPeng is gearing up to roll an "Iron" humanoid robot off the line every two and a half minutes, while Black Friday shoppers have been snapping up half-price robot vacuums from Roborock, Dreame, and Eufy—bringing autonomous hardware straight into the living room.
