Guardrails, Grabs, and Gatekeepers

Who controls our platforms, policies, and payloads today?

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~313 words

The overarching theme of today’s news is a fierce tug-of-war over access, infrastructure, and the rules of the game. In the digital sphere, the European Union is cracking down on addictive algorithms, loot boxes, and influencers with its newly proposed Digital Fairness Act. It is a sharp reminder that maintaining a platform isn’t just about avoiding Monday morning server downtime; it is about defining the legal boundaries of user safety and digital consumption.

Geopolitically, those boundaries are even more contested. Ukraine suspects SpaceX is deliberately throttling its Starshield network to force leverage in peace talks. Whether managing wartime communications, celebrating the NASA Ames scientists of the month, or innovating orbital rideshares—as aerospace firm Moog celebrates 25 years of its ESPA ring—who holds the keys to our critical infrastructure is paramount.

This friction between private power and public welfare is equally visible in healthcare. Pfizer is leaning on private equity behemoth Blackstone to shoulder its massive $10 billion bet on a new monthly obesity drug. As private capital shapes the pharmaceutical market, lawmakers like Bernie Sanders are pushing the opposite direction, launching a coordinated effort to expand Medicare access. Meanwhile, the broader public health conversation continues to fracture, highlighted by the MAHA movement’s controversial alignment with figures like Russell Brand.

We are seeing a similar push for control in our physical and cultural environments. The San Francisco Bay Area just finalized strict new emissions limits that will force a residential heat-pump revolution. In the arts, organizations are fighting to maintain strict custody of their work. Following censorship by Vimeo, Rhizome is partnering with Electronic Arts Intermix to launch a self-hosted video platform. Institutional control is shifting too, with the Warhol Foundation appointing Franklin Sirmans as its new board chair. Yet, physical custody remains entirely precarious—just ask Galerie Ludorff, which watched a $160,000 Jean Arp bronze vanish from its Art Cologne booth in broad daylight.
