The Weight of the Real

Simulated minds distract from the fragile physical world.

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~311 words

We spend an inordinate amount of energy projecting human interiority onto silicon. Even as Anthropic entertains the prospect of AI consciousness—a claim meeting sharp philosophical skepticism—new research suggests that models predicting brain activity may not actually think like human brains at all. In practice, artificial intelligence and robotics remain grounded in stubbornly terrestrial utility: Claude for Manufacturing is busy streamlining engineering quotes, while Cosys warehouse robots ferry spare parts across automotive workshops so mechanics do not have to step away from a lifted car. Perhaps that is why Millennials and analog-craving Gen Zers are returning to the humble, beeping Tamagotchi—a thirty-year-old reminder that we never needed deep cognition to feel a spark of attachment.

Outside the glow of our screens, however, the physical world is proving far more fragile than any simulation. As NASA’s Johnson Space Center marks 65 years of human spaceflight and astronauts study patient-derived gastric microtumors in microgravity aboard the ISS, the orbital commons faces a stark new peril: warnings that Russia could deploy a nuclear-armed satellite sooner than anticipated, threatening thousands of spacecraft in a single blast. Down on Earth, that same destructive impulse is scarring irreplaceable history, with Russian strikes damaging World Heritage sites and cathedrals in Kyiv and Odesa. Against such erasure, the recognition of Brazilian artist Jonathas de Andrade with the 2026 Eye Art & Film Prize feels like a necessary counter-force—a celebration of art that preserves human memory and texture.

Ultimately, our most pressing challenges are biological and civic, not algorithmic. Whether debating how early medical school should begin to bolster pediatric emergency care, or challenging Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and the MAHA movement to prove their pragmatism by championing the MMR vaccine against measles, the mandate is clear. We can automate our workflows and debate machine sentience, but safeguarding our bodies, our skies, and our shared heritage remains an irreducibly human job.
