Photo: Milk Cartons and Bottle Gourds Form Sipho Mabona's Tribute to His Late Father

The vessel does not keep a plate

A father's tribute, and a city's search

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~305 words

Sipho Mabona cuts windows in calabashes and sews them with milk carton foil. The series is The Calabashes We Never Drank Amasi From. His late father, Mongameli Mabona, told of drinking fermented milk from gourds in the Eastern Cape. A small mirror sits near the center of each piece. Close in, the father's voice reads. The vessel keeps a tribute. It does not keep a plate.

On Monday, August 17, 2026, we hold that vessel to one pressure: what we keep as tribute, and what we keep as watch.

Flock runs some 120,000 automatic license plate readers. After officers used such systems to stalk, the company asks for a case number. It does not verify the number. MIT Technology Review says defenders miss the bargain: what is collected, who may search, how long it is kept, how widely it is shared. Verizon asked the Supreme Court for a 47 million dollar refund of an FCC fine. The Court said no. Carriers still say selling device location is not illegal. The plate is kept. The person is not returned.

HEO will use Planet satellites to photograph other spacecraft. NASA's network over Addis Ababa gives a long view of black carbon from fires, diesel, and other combustion. Lattice's Karl Wachswender says the intelligence of robots sits less in the model than in the sensors underneath. The sky is a sensor. The street is a sensor.

Care loses another kind of sight. Eyepoint's experimental treatment for wet AMD failed to keep vision with fewer injections than the standard. Kindergarten vaccination rates fall again as nonmedical exemptions rise. The CDC put the numbers online and skipped a full report.

Convergence is not a thicker camera. It is whether the keep we make, in a gourd or a clinic, still belongs to the living and not only to the watch.
