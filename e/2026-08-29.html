Jerusalem in the sack is live

Hay and henna, a lock you can leave off

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~314 words

Sliman Mansour painted an elderly man staggering, Jerusalem stuffed in the sack on his back, the golden dome in the load. The 1973 canvas is Jamal al Mahamel, Camel of Hardship. He was born in Birzeit on July 27, 1947. He died in Jerusalem on August 24, at 79, after a long illness.

On Saturday, August 29, 2026, we hold that sack to one pressure: a city that is still carried, and a lock that now claims the habit is finished.

He was jailed four or five times, his canvases seized. In 1989 he founded New Visions with Nabil Anani, Vera Tamari, and Tayseer Barakat. They put down imported paint and took up mud, coffee, henna, and hay. The land became the pigment.

On Friday the FDA cleared Juul2, the first new device in more than a decade. An app can lock it until age is verified. The lock is optional. Smoking used to be everywhere the way the feeds are now. Society decided to break the habit. It took a long time. Seventeen billion is not the end of the feed.

The split, regulators say, belongs at the Supreme Court. In Houston the Artemis II crew received the Congressional Space Medal of Honor: Victor Glover, Christina Koch, Jeremy Hansen, Reid Wiseman. Convergence is not a medal. It is whether the city is still carried in the hay, and whether a lock you can leave off still has to answer for the young.
