Systems of Control and Chaos

Navigating unpredictability in technology, biology, and the art world.

By Dr. Wallace Lynch · Editor in Chief · ~324 words

We live in an era obsessed with engineering predictability out of chaos. In the aerospace sector, Airbus is addressing the tumbling crisis of dead satellites that threaten orbit with a new passive spin stabilization solution. Down on Earth, executives and engineers alike are wrestling with system control. Apple is strategically capitalizing on the artificial intelligence boom without busting its budget on the massive infrastructure spending consuming its rivals. Conversely, Google recently had to dismantle a backdoor after an Android manufacturing partner secretly turned 1.3 million set-top boxes into a massive botnet.

This friction between the wild and the programmed extends to our physical environments. On the factory floor, experts are beginning to question the limits of automation, exploring what happens when an industrial robot is mathematically correct but an experienced human operator believes the machine is wrong. In neuroscience, researchers examining Belyaev's famous Siberian silver foxes discovered that domestication biologically pruned brain circuits, leaving the friendly animals with fewer estrogen receptor alpha neurons. Yet, we cannot entirely control nature; this vulnerability was tragically underscored by the recent death of a Washington state resident from bird flu following exposure to infected cattle.

Biological and systemic uncertainties will soon meet political reality, as lobbyists and experts attempt to predict how the upcoming midterms will reshape American health policy. Similarly, solar and wind developers are maneuvering to hold onto the long-term federal tax incentives established by the 2022 Inflation Reduction Act.

Even the art world is entangled in this web of unpredictability and administrative friction. Conceptual artist Mary Ellen Carroll has built a practice out of bureaucratic maneuvers and red tape, while exhibitors at Art Basel Paris are actively demanding compensation after security concerns over protests forced the fair to close two days early. Ultimately, whether we are analyzing modern policy or the profound impact of Marcel Duchamp’s impossible love for sculptor Maria Martins on his work, human unpredictability remains the one variable we cannot engineer away.
